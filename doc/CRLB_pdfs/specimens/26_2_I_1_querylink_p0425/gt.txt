The British Museum drawing of Nessus and
Deianeira is extraordinary for the almost palpitat-
ing sense of life that Rubens gave to the figures,
and the humane and intimate way in which they
are shown clinging together. The design of this
composition has been given to Perino del Vaga
on the basis of a seventeenth-century inscription
written at the lower left. No related work has so
far been identified, but some specialists in Ital-
ian drawing have accepted that the composition
could indeed be his.1 The main area of disagree-
ment has been over whether this is a work entirely
